Pre-Foreclosure Call Checklist

Help first. Be honest. The deal follows the trust.

Matches the promises on our website: no pressure, every option explained, everything in writing.

Give your name, say why you're calling, and ask if now is a good time. Make it clear this is a conversation, not a pitch.

"Hi, is this Maria? My name's Julien, I'm with French Equity Partners here in Tampa. I saw there's a foreclosure filing on your home, and I'm calling to see if it would help to talk through your options. No obligation at all. Is now an OK time?"
HigherCalm and friendly, clear about who you are, easy for them to say "not now."
LowerVague about who you are, rushing, homeowner feels ambushed.

Ask something simple and personal, like how long they've lived there. Match their energy. Normalize their situation:

"A lot of people I speak with got here through a job change or a medical bill. It happens to good people."

Only tell stories that are true. Until you have your own, speak generally rather than inventing a case.

HigherGenuine connection, homeowner relaxes.
LowerJumping straight to business, or telling a made-up story.

Your role

"I'm a private buyer. I'm not an agent, lender, or attorney."

What the call is for

"I'll go through every option you have, including the ones that don't involve me. If buying your house doesn't make sense, I'll tell you and point you to someone who can help."

The timeline, gently

"The court process does have deadlines, so it's worth understanding your options now, while you have the most of them."
HigherHonest framing, urgency grounded in their real timeline, guard comes down.
LowerMentioning "underwriters" or "programs," or creating fear.
  • Who is on title, and who needs to sign: spouse, ex-spouse, heirs?
  • Any second mortgage, HOA debt, tax liens, judgments, or solar loans?
  • If they're open to it, ask permission to request a payoff statement from their lender. They'll need to sign an authorization form.
HigherFull picture, no surprises at closing.
LowerDiscovering a co-owner or a lien at the end.
"What would you like to happen? Keep the house, or are you open to selling?"

Then listen. Find out:

  • how they fell behind and how far behind they are
  • what the monthly payment is
  • whether their income has stabilized
  • how long they've owned the home
  • what condition it's in
HigherYou talk less than they do, and their real goal is clear before you present anything.
LowerAssuming they want to sell, or skipping the money questions.

If they want to keep the house

  • Reinstatement: works if they can gather the lump sum.
  • Loan modification or repayment plan: strong if their income has stabilized. Apply early with a complete application.
  • Bankruptcy: Chapter 13 can genuinely save a home. Refer them to an attorney.
  • Give them the free HUD counselor number: 800-569-4287.

The goal is for them to understand their situation, not to steer them toward selling.

If they want or need to sell

  • Listing with an agent: usually the most money if they have equity and time.
  • Short sale: if they owe more than the house is worth.
  • Cash sale to us: speed, as-is, and certainty of closing.
HigherThey make an informed choice, and you'd be comfortable with them repeating everything you said to an attorney.
LowerDownplaying options that don't benefit you.
"Here's how I get to a number, so there are no surprises. I look at what similar homes nearby sold for once fixed up, subtract repairs, and leave room for the costs of reselling. I only offer a number I can actually close at, because a high number that falls apart right before your sale date is the worst thing that could happen to you."
HigherThe process is transparent, and the offer will land with credibility.
LowerThrowing out a number with no explanation.
  • What are they hoping to walk away with?
  • What matters most: price, timing, or certainty?
  • Do they need help with the move, or extra time before moving out?
"Before I run the numbers, I want to know what you actually need, so I can see whether I can get there."
HigherTheir needs are on the table first.
LowerAnchoring them with your number first.
  • Don't invent a hold. Say: "Let me run the numbers properly. I'll call you back within the hour." Then actually call back.
  • Walk them through the math: after-repair value, repairs, costs, their payoff, and roughly what they'd walk away with.
  • If listing would clearly net them more, say so.
  • Agree on a next step: "I'll email the written offer tonight. Please look it over with family or an attorney, and I'll call you Thursday."
  • Never ask for a signature on the first call. When they do sign, point out their cancellation right under Florida law.
HigherClear offer, math shown, next step agreed, no pressure.
LowerFake urgency, pushing for a signature today.

Find out why: the price, the timing, or trust.

  • If it's the price and your numbers allow it, improve the offer, up to your maximum offer and no further.
  • If you still can't meet their needs, point them to the better path: a good local agent, a short sale, or legal aid.
"If anything changes, call me. I'm happy to help either way."
HigherThey end the call feeling helped, even with no deal. Those people come back and refer others.
LowerArguing, going above your numbers, or burning the relationship.
  • Acknowledge: "That makes complete sense."
  • Understand: "Can I ask what's making you hesitate?"
  • Help: answer honestly, then suggest a next step.

"I need to think about it" is a fair answer. Book a follow-up call rather than pushing through it. A helpful question:

"What would you like things to look like 60 days from now?"
HigherThey feel heard, and the conversation keeps moving respectfully.
Lower"Overcoming" people, or treating hesitation as something to beat.
  • Log your notes, their deadlines, and the follow-up date.
  • Text or email them the help page, frenchequitypartners.com/foreclosure-help.html, and the counselor number.
  • Follow up when you said you would.

Never say

  • "My underwriters" or "our programs"
  • "I'm not sure you'll qualify"
  • "I can stop your foreclosure"
  • "You need to sign today"
  • Anything that requires them to pay you
